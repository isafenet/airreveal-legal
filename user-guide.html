User guide

Getting the most from AirReveal

For AirReveal 3.0 on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch · Updated October 2026

New to AirReveal? You only need four steps. Add your flight, let it download while you have Wi-Fi, start Live at the airport, then look out of the window. On the ground, the map shows the planes flying over you, the places around you and every airport. Everything below is optional extra. The same guide is in the app, under Profile › Settings › Help.

1Quick start

New to AirReveal? You only need four steps. Everything else in this guide is optional extra.

  1. Add your flight. On the Map, tap Add a Flight, type your flight number and tap Find, or enter the airports yourself. Touch and hold Add a Flight to add flights from your calendar. Your flight's airport then glows gold on the map with a countdown.
  2. Get it ready. While you still have Wi-Fi, the flight's maps and landmarks download, so they work in the air. On the day, your flight's card on the map shows when they're ready, and an amber warning sits on the map until they are.
  3. Start Live. At the airport, tap Start Live. AirReveal asks to use your location, then for Always, so it keeps following the plane while your phone is locked.
  4. Look out of the window. Airplane mode is fine: your phone's GPS works without a signal. AirReveal tells you what's below and which side to look.

You don't need in-flight Wi-Fi, a mobile signal or an account. On the ground, the Map shows the planes flying over you, the airports and the places around you.

2The Map

The map is where AirReveal opens. It tells the story of what's above you and what's below:

  • Above you: the planes in the sky around you, when you have a signal. Each plane's icon shows what kind it is. Tap one for its card: what it is, where it's going and Follow. Swipe the card up for all of it (the photo, the route, the aircraft and your logbook) and down to close it. A radar sweeps round from your dot: tap the dot to stop or start it.
  • Below you: the landmarks near you, with or without a signal. Tap one for its story.
  • Airports: every airport shows as a small blue plane with its code: the big hubs from anywhere, and every airfield as you zoom in. Tap one for its card.
  • Your flights: your next flight's airport glows gold with a countdown. Tap it and its route draws itself across the map, with Fly the Route Now and its Flight Page. Your Flights shows all of yours as routes on the map, and All Flights lists every one.
  • The buttons at the top right: your Profile, the camera (see The camera), the compass, which turns the map with you so what's ahead is at the top (tap it again for north up), and On the Map, for the planes, places and radar and the kind of map. Moved the map away? The compass shows an arrow: tap it and the map glides back to you.
  • The header at the top left says how many planes are above and places below. Tap it for every plane, nearest first. After a few seconds it folds into a small button so the map has the screen.
  • Add a Flight is at the foot of the map. Touch and hold it to add flights from your calendar, or to try the sample flight.
  • Following a plane: tap Follow on its card and the map keeps it in the middle, with its trail and its airports. A pill at the top says you're following it: tap it for its card, with Stop Following.
  • Quiet where you are? Tap an airport, then Look Around Here (part of Live Sky in AirReveal Pro): the map moves there and shows its planes and places, as far as your Sky Range and Discovery Range reach. A gold pill at the top says where you're looking, with Back to Me; the arrow button brings you back too. Alerts and the camera stay with you.
  • On the day, your flight's card has Start Live. After a few seconds it folds into a Start Live button so the map has the screen: tap it to start, or touch and hold it for more (Hold for More, then Hold for Less to fold it again). Until your maps are downloaded, an amber warning on the map shows what to do. In the air the same map follows your plane, with Window, Pilot, Runway and Sky to open.

On iPad, and on the iPhone Duo opened out, the planes around you and the places below are listed beside the map.

3Adding a flight

Add a Flight asks where you're flying From and To. The rest is optional: Airline, Flight Number, Aircraft Type and Scheduled Departure. If your airline or aircraft isn't listed, type it in.

Choose Add As: a Live Trip for a flight you're taking, followed by GPS, or a Planned Trip to explore a route ahead of time. A flight that leaves more than 6 hours from now is added as planned, and you start it Live from the map on the day.

Your first Live-tracked flight is free. After that, AirReveal Pro keeps Live tracking going on every flight. Planning and previewing are always free.

Been sent a flight by a friend, or saved one with Export? Choose Import at the top of Add a Flight and pick the file.

4Before you fly

Best Side to Sit. Every flight tells you whether to sit on the left or the right, the sights you'll see from each side, and where the sunrise or sunset will be. It's worked out on your phone from the route and your departure time, so treat it as a good bet rather than a promise.

Maps for the flight. A flight's maps and landmarks download on Wi-Fi by themselves (turn Download Maps on Wi-Fi on or off in Profile › Settings › Your Flight Options). In My Flights, a flight shows Available offline once its maps are ready. A download that stopped carries on where it left off.

On the day. Your flight's card on the map has a checklist: Maps for the route downloaded and Location while you fly. When both are done it says Ready for Take-off. Until then, an amber warning on the map shows what's left to do.

Explore the route. Open a planned flight and tap Explore the Route to fly it on the map before you go. Drag along the progress bar to jump anywhere, or tap play.

5On board: the Live map

The Live map shows where you are, a headline such as "Over Lake Baikal" and how far there is to go.

  • Airplane mode is fine. GPS reads satellites, not phone masts. After take-off, or deep in the cabin, it can take a minute to find you: hold your phone near the window.
  • Your flight sits at the top of the map: the route, the time left and your height. Tap it for all the details; touch and hold it for the Flight Timeline, Discovery Bingo, the Live Activity and the map menu. The panel below has the place to look at now, Window, Pilot, Runway and Sky, and what's coming up; pull it up for more, push it down for more map. After a few seconds it folds into a round button with a gold ring of how far along; tap it to see the flight again. Drag the map to look around and the panel tucks down; Back to Your Plane brings you back.
  • Flight Details has the weather below, with Golden Hour, and the Flight Timeline of take-off, sunsets, border crossings and landing.
  • Which side to look. Landmarks and the sun are marked left or right, as you face the front of the plane.
  • Planes near yours. With Wi-Fi on board, the other planes around yours appear on the map, gliding along their tracks; the nearest say how far above or below you they are. Tap one for its card. Offline, the map simply shows your flight and the landmarks below. Live Monitoring (Profile › Settings › The Map › Planes Above You) turns it off.
  • The map menu (touch and hold the flight at the top of the map, or Map in your flight's details) has the Camera: Flat, 3D Flyover, Cockpit (looking ahead from about the plane's height) and Window (looking out of the left or right window, with a switch on screen). It also has Map Style, Show on Map (cities, landmarks, mountains, rivers and lakes) and Zoom. Your choice of camera is remembered, and it works offline.
  • Start Live Activity puts your progress on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.
  • Discovery Bingo gives each flight a card of challenges from your own route.
  • On iPad, the sidebar has the tabs and, under This flight, the Live Map and its views; Window, Pilot and Runway View open in the map's place. Beside the map are Pilot View's way ahead, what's coming up and on which side, and the weather where you land; drag along The whole flight at the foot of the map to look ahead or back. An open iPhone Duo has the same with a rail of icons, or, held upright, the map above the fold and your flight below. Closed, it shows the iPhone layout with the tabs down the right-hand side.

Always follow the crew. AirReveal is for enjoying the journey, not an official flight-status or safety service.

6Window View, photos and videos

Tap Window on your flight's panel (on iPad, Window View in the sidebar) and hold your phone up to the window: the landmarks outside are labelled on the camera picture, from mountain tops to cities and lakes. Aim at one and the card at the bottom names it; tap a label or the card for its story. It works offline.

With Wi-Fi on board, the other planes near yours are marked too, with how far above or below you they are. Tap one for its card. Offline, Window View shows the landmarks downloaded with your flight.

  • Left and Right at the top switch between the left and right window; on iPad, the card beside them says which way your window faces. Touch and hold Left and Right for the compass, Recenter (for labels that have drifted), the horizon line and About this view. ✕ closes the view.
  • The controls fade after a few seconds so you can see out. Tap anywhere to bring them back.

Along the bottom, Moments opens your photos and videos in Photos, the big shutter takes a photo and the red button records. Touch and hold the red button to choose Video or Timelapse:

  • Take Photo saves the picture with its labels and a caption to Photos.
  • Record Video records up to 5 minutes, with the labels on every frame.
  • Record Timelapse takes a frame every 2 seconds and plays it back 60 times faster, for up to 3 hours. The screen stays on while it records.

Everything is saved to your Photos, ready to share. Nothing is recorded unless you take a photo, video or timelapse, and a recording still going is saved when you close the view. The same choices are in the camera on the map, for the planes overhead.

7Pilot View

Tap Pilot on your flight's panel (on iPad, Pilot View in the sidebar) for the way ahead as if from the flight deck: the sky, the horizon on the curved Earth, where you're heading as a bright magenta line and your planned route as a faint dashed one, your destination and the places ahead, with glass tapes for ground speed, altitude and heading. Your downloaded map is the ground (tap the map button for a grid instead), and the forecast cloud along your route is drawn at its real heights, fetched while you have signal and kept for the air.

  • The tapes read your phone's GPS: real altitude, speed and heading. A cabin gives the GPS few fixes, so between them the aircraft is carried forward at its last speed, and an Estimated caption says so until the next fix. The map, Lock Screen and Watch do the same.
  • The airport ahead: on take-off and approach Pilot View draws the runways with their markings and numbers, and the terminal buildings. The altitude tape marks the airport's ground, with your height above it below 5,000 ft.
  • Turns tilt the horizon, as the aircraft banks, with the angle on the scale at the top; the heading follows the track you're actually flying, vectors and all.
  • Sideways gives the view the whole screen. Tap ⓘ for a reminder of what's what.

Profile › Settings › Look and Feel › Appearance › Opening chooses how AirReveal starts: clouds parting, flying through them, or straight in. Tracking Diary (Profile › Settings › Your Flight Options › Trip Data) keeps what live tracking did on each flight; share it with us if a flight didn't track as it should.

8Runway View

Tap Runway on your flight's panel (on iPad, Runway View in the sidebar) to watch your own plane from outside, on a model of the airport: it taxis, takes off and lands at its GPS position, on the real runways with their markings and numbers, among the terminal, hangars and tower.

  • Three cameras: Spotter, beside the runway; Chase, behind the plane; Tower, from the control tower. Drag to look around and pinch to zoom.
  • In its airline's colours. Your plane is painted in its airline's colours, with the airline's code on the fin (never its logo), at the size of its type, and its lights and wheels work as on the real one. Buildings are drawn at their mapped heights, and where the map has no control tower, a standard one stands beside the terminal for the Tower camera.
  • Works offline. The runways come with the app, and the airport's buildings and taxiways download with your flight's offline map, so add the flight while you have signal. Where the map has no buildings for an airport, you'll see the runways only.
  • The readout shows your speed, height above the airport and the runway you're on or nearest.
  • Above the route. Once you're through 3,000 ft (or 8 km out), Runway View rises from the airport to a view above your route on the map, with the forecast cloud around your plane and a contrail at height. Pick Follow, Chase or Above. The line along the bottom names what's below, then your height, speed, the cloud and the time to go. Within 15 km of landing it drops back to the airport.
  • Smooth, and on the taxiways. Your plane is drawn a second behind its GPS so it glides rather than jumps: it lifts its nose for take-off, flares to land, banks in turns and, on the ground, keeps to the taxiway and runway centre lines. In a rehearsal it taxis on the airport's real taxiways, with the way to the runway or the stand drawn in magenta and the taxiways' letters in the readout.

9Explore: what's below

The Explore tab has two parts:

  • On Your Flight: what's around you. The place to look at now is the big picture at the top, marked Look now with the side of the plane (touch and hold it for which side to look for it and for the sun). The chips above it show just one kind of place: All, Cities, Mountains and the rest on your route. Below it come picture tiles with the side to look and how many minutes away: what's overhead first, then what's coming up, soonest first, then the places you've flown past, dimmed. Tap a place for What You're Seeing (written on your iPhone by Apple Intelligence where it's available), the Encyclopedia article, Read Aloud, which side to look, and Start Quiz. On iPad, the place opens beside the tiles.
  • Landmark Quiz: the teal tile starts a quiz about the place to look at now (it shows while quizzes are on in Settings › Games). On iPad, use Start Quiz on the place's page.
  • Your Collection: every landmark along your routes, from mountains, rivers and islands to volcanoes, cities and UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Places you haven't discovered stay hidden until you fly past them.
  • Photos: the best-known landmarks have a photo, credited to its photographer (they're all listed in Data and Credits). In Your Collection, places you haven't discovered stay blurred until you fly past them. Other places get a photo from Wikimedia Commons when Wikipedia has one, credited on the place's page and kept on your phone for the air (photos for a flight's landmarks download with its maps on Wi-Fi). A place's page also brings its Wikipedia article when you're online, and keeps it for next time.

Planning a flight? Explore shows that route's places, with a banner at the top: tap it to go back to your real flight.

10Planes above you

With a signal, the map shows the planes flying around you, with rings every 10 miles (or 10 km). Without AirReveal Pro they update every minute; with Live Sky in Pro, every few seconds.

  • Each plane's icon shows what kind it is: an airliner, a turboprop, a business jet, a light aircraft, a fast jet or a helicopter.
  • Tap a plane to reveal it: its airline, route, height, speed and heading, and a photo of the aircraft when one is known. Every plane you reveal goes in your Spotter's Logbook (Sky Logbook in your Profile).
  • Swipe the card up for the aircraft: its photo and specs, such as top speed, cruising height, range, seats, wingspan, engines and first flight.
  • Follow, on a plane's card, follows it on the map for its whole flight, however far it goes: it stays in the middle with its trail, and a pill at the top shows you're following (tap it for its card). Touch and hold Follow to follow it in the camera instead. Out of range of the receivers it waits where it was last heard, and on the ground it has landed. You'll see it on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island too. Stop Following, or the arrow button back to you, ends it.
  • Tell Me When It Lands sends a notification when a plane you follow lands, even with AirReveal closed.
  • Police, air ambulance, coastguard, firefighting and military aircraft show in their own colours, and you can have an alert when one is near.
  • The day's free planes. The free planes come from a community feed with a daily limit. If it runs out, the header says Planes back at and the time; the places, airports and your flights keep working. Live Sky has no daily limit.

How far AirReveal looks is the Sky Range, in Profile › Settings › The Map › Planes Above You. Turn Live Monitoring off there if you'd rather it didn't watch the sky at all. Positions come from ADSB.lol, and routes can be out of date.

11Pilot View from a plane in the sky

Tap a plane on the map, then Sit in Its Cockpit (part of Live Sky in AirReveal Pro), to see the way ahead from its captain's seat: its flight deck around you, its route ahead, and its instruments reading what the plane itself sends.

  • View chooses Cockpit, + HUD (the cockpit with its head-up display), HUD or Gauges (Pilot View's own instruments): at the top on iPhone, in the pane beside the cockpit on iPad. The HUD is the green head-up display on the windscreen: speed and altitude, the horizon, the heading and the autopilot's modes.
  • Drag to look around the flight deck: left, right, up and down. Double-tap, or tap Look Ahead, to face forward again.
  • Tap a screen on the flight deck, such as the Primary Flight Display or the Navigation Display, to see it big. It keeps showing the plane's live readings. On iPhone, swipe down or tap Close to put it back; on iPad it shows in the pane beside the cockpit (the Navigation Display until you tap another), above What it's sending: its airspeed, altitude, heading, autopilot and bank.
  • EST marks a reading the plane doesn't send, which AirReveal works out instead, such as how far it's banking. If the plane goes quiet, its position is carried forward until it's heard again.
  • Follow All the Way keeps following the plane after it leaves your range.
  • Watch It Fly, beside Sit in Its Cockpit, shows the plane in 3D, as Runway View shows your own flight: at an airport as it taxis, takes off and lands (Spotter, Chase, Tower), and in the air from behind or above over the map, along its route (Follow, Chase, Above), in its airline's colours. A plane whose route isn't known is shown over the map only. It uses the same connection as the cockpit.

The cockpits and Watch It Fly need the internet and AirReveal Pro. They ask for the plane no more than every 10 seconds, and less often when the network is busy.

12The camera

Tap the camera button at the top right of the map and hold your phone up: the planes in the sky are labelled with their callsigns, each with a little plane pointing the way it flies, and the landmarks around you are labelled in orange on the horizon, with how far away they are. Aim at one and the card at the bottom says what it is.

  • Tap a label or the card to reveal the plane, or for a landmark's story.
  • No signal? The landmarks are on your phone, so they're still labelled; the planes need a connection. In the air, Sky on the Live map opens the same camera.
  • The compass at the top shows every aircraft around you: teal ones are in the picture, white ones are a turn away, and the one you're following is amber. The arrows at the sides of the screen say how many more are to your left or right.
  • Line Up, in the bar at the bottom: if the labels sit a little to one side of the real planes, drag them into place. Every label moves together, and it lasts until you close the camera.
  • If the compass's ring turns amber, it's unsettled: move your phone in a figure of eight, away from metal.
  • Photo, Video and Timelapse work here too, with the labels on the picture.
  • Guess the Airline turns the planes overhead into a game (see the next section).
  • The controls fade after a few seconds so you can see the sky. Tap anywhere to bring them back. Hold your phone sideways and they move to the side. Tap About this view (ⓘ) for help.

13Guess the Airline

A game for one or two people in the camera: tap Guess the Airline in the bar at the bottom. Planes with an airline show a question mark: tap one, guess who's flying it from the choices, then see if you were right.

  • Players: One or Two. Two players take turns on one iPhone, passing it over between planes. Names are optional and stay on your iPhone.
  • Hints: Show the Tail Colours or Where's It Flying? cost some of a right answer's points.
  • Easy Mode: three airlines to choose from instead of four, and free hints. Good for younger players.
  • Keep Airlines a Mystery (Profile › Settings › Games): tap any plane, on the map too, and guess its airline before its card opens.
  • At the end, see the scores, Play Again or Share Scores. Right answers count for XP, a daily challenge and two achievements.

14Your Logbook, games and achievements

AirReveal turns your flights, and the sky above you, into a game. All of it is optional.

  • Spotter's Logbook (Sky Logbook in your Profile): every aircraft you've revealed, today's spotting, your Personal Bests (highest, fastest, closest, longest follow), the aircraft types and airlines you've seen, and countries of registration.
  • Today's Challenges: three new ones every day at midnight, such as spotting a helicopter.
  • Sky Bingo: nine squares to spot from home or from your seat. A full card starts a new one. Each flight also has its own Discovery Bingo from its route.
  • Quizzes: open any landmark and tap Start Quiz for three quick questions. Each aircraft has trivia too.
  • Achievements, levels and XP: badges such as Globetrotter and Mountaineer, and rare and legendary aircraft to find.
  • Passport: a stamp for every country you fly over, even if you never land there.

Prefer a clean flight tracker? Turn any of them off in Profile › Settings › Games. Your Logbook, Collection and Passport stay, and progress keeps counting so nothing is lost if you turn them back on.

15Spotter Alerts

Get a notification when an aircraft you're watching for comes near, even with AirReveal closed. Turn it on in Profile › Settings › Alerts › Spotter Alerts.

  • Watch For: choose from the list, such as the Airbus A380, Boeing 747s, the Airbus Beluga, the Boeing 747 Dreamlifter or the Antonov An-124, or add any aircraft type code. Up to 12.
  • Alert Distance: from 5 to 100 miles.
  • One alert per aircraft in 3 hours, so a plane circling nearby doesn't keep buzzing.

To check while the app is closed, your location, rounded to about a kilometre, is kept on iSafeNet's server with these settings, and checked every 2 minutes. Nothing else is sent, and turning Spotter Alerts off deletes it.

16After you land

  • Welcome to your destination. When you land, AirReveal shows Your Flight, Revealed: the countries you crossed, how far you flew and the sights you spotted. Tap Share Your Recap for a picture card.
  • Your journal is already done. Completed flights are saved automatically.
  • Flying on? Your next flight is waiting on the map, glowing gold, with the best side to sit.
  • Keep it safe. iCloud Sync copies your flights to your other Apple devices, and Export All Trip Data (Profile › Settings › Your Flight Options › Trip Data) saves a backup file.

17My Flights

My Flights (All Flights under Your Flights on the map, or the Journal's filter button) lists every flight: Planned, Live and Completed. Search them, filter by status or airline, and sort by date, route, airline, flight number or the longest flight.

  • Swipe a flight to download its maps, export it as a file to share, or remove it.
  • Your Flying at the top adds up everything you've flown (see below).
  • Tap + for a New Flight, to Import Past Flights or to Add From Calendar.

18Import Past Flights and Add From Calendar

Import Past Flights brings in the flights you've already flown, from the file another app exports:

  • Flighty: Settings › Account Data › Export Your Flights.
  • myFlightradar24: sign in at my.flightradar24.com, open Settings › Export and download the CSV file.
  • OpenFlights: on openflights.org, Your Flights › Export.
  • Anything else: a CSV file with a date and From and To airport codes, one flight a line.

Each flight is added as a completed flight, never twice, and your flight log and Your Flying fill up straight away. The file is read on your iPhone and nothing is sent anywhere. Imported flights don't earn XP or achievements: those come from flights AirReveal follows.

Add From Calendar looks through your calendar, from two years ago to a year ahead, for events that name two airports (like LHR → JFK) or a flight number (like BA 117). Choose the ones to add: upcoming flights become planned flights, and past ones go into your flight log. Your calendar is read on your iPhone, and nothing from it is kept or sent anywhere except the flights you add.

19Your Flying

Your Flying (at the top of My Flights) adds up every completed flight, including any you've imported: how many flights, the distance, time in the air, airports, countries, airlines and aircraft types, your longest flight, how many times you've flown around the world and your most-visited airports, with a map of all your routes.

See All Time, or choose a Year.

20The Journal

The Journal tab keeps every flight's story: your flights, countries and landmarks at the top (tap them for your Passport), then a trip in progress, marked Live, and your completed trips under the month or season they were flown (older ones under their year), each with a picture, its date and how many countries and landmarks. The round button at the top searches, filters, sorts and groups them, and opens My Flights and your Passport. To delete a trip, swipe it left or touch and hold it.

Open a trip for its route on a map, the distance, countries, landmarks and time, its Highlights, the countries you Crossed, and your highest altitude and speed. Replay This Flight plays it back, and Share Trip Recap makes a picture card of your route, distance, time and discoveries. On iPad, the trip opens beside the list.

21Apple Watch, widgets and the Lock Screen

  • Apple Watch: the Above You radar (turn the Digital Crown to zoom), the plane you're following with an arrow pointing to it, and your own flight. Add AirReveal to a watch face or the Smart Stack.
  • Widgets: Above You on the Home Screen and Lock Screen, with a small radar. Page through the aircraft with the up and down buttons.
  • Live Activity: your flight, or the plane you're following, on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.

22Siri and Shortcuts

Ask Siri, without opening the app:

  • "How long left on my AirReveal flight": when the flight you're following is due, and how long that is.
  • "What's below me in AirReveal": the country and the nearest places below you. It uses AirReveal's offline places, so it works in the air.
  • "What's flying above me in AirReveal": the nearest aircraft, its type, height and route when known, how far away it is, and how many more are nearby.

You'll find them in the Shortcuts app too.

23Making it yours

Tap the round button at the top right of the Map or Explore for your Profile (on iPad, Profile at the foot of the sidebar): your Passport card (your name, how far you've flown, countries and badges; tap your name to change it), then Yours (Achievements, with your level and XP, and your Sky Logbook) and Settings, starting with AirReveal Pro.

Settings lists each group, which opens its own page:

  • The Map: Map Look (each look as a little map, Night Mode, and the planes, places and radar on the map), Planes Above You (Live Monitoring, the Sky Range, emergency and military aircraft, Keep Airlines a Mystery), Places Below (the Discovery Range) and your Units.
  • Your Flight Options: the Flight HUD, Download Maps on Wi-Fi and Trip Data. Alerts: Spotter Alerts, Tell Me When It Lands and emergency aircraft near you.
  • Games, then Look and Feel: Appearance (the skin, Light or Dark, the opening and the app icon), Color Theme and Language.
  • iCloud Sync, Help, and About, which has Show Welcome Tour Again.

24AirReveal Free and Pro

Free: plan and preview as many flights as you like, fully offline, plus your very first Live-tracked flight. The planes above you update every minute, and you can follow one and see them through the camera. The places below, the airports, the Logbook and the games are free too.

The free planes come from a community feed with a daily limit. If it runs out, the map says when they're back; the places, airports and your flights keep working.

AirReveal Pro adds Live Sky: the planes every few seconds with no daily limit, Watch It Fly and the cockpits, and Look Around at any airport. It also adds Live GPS tracking of your real flight on every flight, including offline maps while you fly. Choose weekly, monthly or yearly in Profile › AirReveal Pro.

Bought Pro but it isn't showing? Tap Restore Purchases there.

25Words you'll see

GPS: the satellite signal your phone uses to work out where you are. It works in airplane mode.

Altitude and ft: how high the plane is, in feet. Airliners usually cruise at 30,000 to 40,000 ft, which is 9 to 12 km.

Ground Speed and kt: how fast you're moving over the ground. Pilots use knots: 1 kt is about 1.85 km/h or 1.15 mph.

Heading: the compass direction the plane is pointing: 0° north, 90° east, 180° south, 270° west.

HUD: head-up display, the panel over the map with your height, speed and heading.

Callsign: the name a plane uses on the radio, such as BAW117 for a British Airways flight. It's often the flight number with the airline's three-letter code.

Aircraft type: the model of plane, such as A320 or 787. The short code (like A388 for the Airbus A380) is its type code.

Airport codes (LHR, JFK): the three letters on your boarding pass. Airline codes (BA, EK) start your flight number.

Golden Hour: the hour after sunrise or before sunset, when the light is soft and warm.

Live Trip and Planned Trip: a flight you're taking now, followed by GPS, or one you're exploring ahead of time.

Reveal: tapping a plane on the map or in the camera to see who and what it is. It's logged in your Spotter's Logbook.

Sky Range and Discovery Range: how far AirReveal looks for planes around you, and for places around your plane.

XP: experience points for discoveries, quizzes, Bingo and flights. They raise your level.

UNESCO: the United Nations body that names World Heritage Sites.

26If something isn't working

The plane isn't moving on the map. Check that location is allowed in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services › AirReveal, then hold your phone near the window for a minute.

No maps or landmarks in the air. The flight probably wasn't downloaded before take-off. You can still follow along. Next time, let it download on Wi-Fi first.

No planes on the map. The planes need a connection, and Live Monitoring on in Profile › Settings › The Map › Planes Above You. Over quiet country there may simply be nothing near: with AirReveal Pro, try Look Around Here at a busy airport.

The header says Planes back at… The day's free planes are used up, and they come back at that time. Live Sky in AirReveal Pro has no daily limit.

The labels in the camera are off to one side. Use Line Up to drag them onto the planes, or move your phone in a figure of eight, away from metal.

No alerts or landing notifications. Allow notifications in Settings › Apps › AirReveal › Notifications.

Pro isn't showing after you bought it. Tap Restore Purchases in Profile › AirReveal Pro.

Still stuck? Email info@isafenet.app, or see Support in Profile › Settings › About.

27Where the data comes from

AirReveal is built on open data: maps from OpenStreetMap (via OpenFreeMap), places from Overture Maps and Natural Earth, airports from OpenFlights, landmark articles from Wikipedia, live aircraft positions from ADSB.lol, aircraft details from adsbdb, routes from Virtual Radar Server's standing data, photos from Planespotters.net and Wikimedia Commons, and weather from Apple Weather.

Airport elevations and runways come from OurAirports, and airport buildings, aprons and taxiways from OpenStreetMap.

Every source and its licence is in Profile › Settings › About › Data and Credits.
